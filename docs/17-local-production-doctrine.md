# 17. The local production doctrine

> *"Some of those commodities can be farmed indoors — petrol, oil, gas, even
> medicine. Add this important doctrine. How can we fix this?"*

**This substantially fixes the problem §16 left open**, and it does so by
correcting an error in §16's own analysis.

---

## 17.1 The error being corrected

§16 treated "medicines" as one category, found a ~200-year substitution
payback, and concluded that lethal imports cannot be localised. That
conclusion was **too coarse and too pessimistic.**

"Medicine" spans:

| Product | Reality |
|---|---|
| Oral rehydration salts | Sugar, salt, clean water |
| Medical oxygen | **Made from air** |
| Ethanol disinfectant | Ferment and distil |
| IV saline | Salt and water — but sterility is unforgiving |
| Paracetamol tablets | Formulating an imported active ingredient |
| The active ingredient itself | Industrial chemistry at national scale |
| Vaccines | Not achievable at district scale, at all |

Lumping these together produced a wrong answer. Disaggregated, **some of the
most life-critical items are among the easiest to produce locally.**

Medical oxygen is the decisive example. Pressure-swing adsorption plants are
running at district scale in Kenya, Rwanda and Ethiopia; a facility serving
150–200 beds costs roughly **US$100–110k**, and measured whole-life cost has
come in near **US$7.34 per patient treated**. Oxygen is separated from the
atmosphere.

**Air cannot be embargoed, sanctioned, or priced in dollars.**

---

## 17.2 The production ladder

The organising principle: rank by **technical difficulty**, not by
importance. A community climbs in order, because attempting a rung before
mastering the one below wastes capital and discredits the programme.

| Rung | Type | Requires |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Grow / gather | land, water, seed, knowledge |
| **2** | Simple processing | presses, driers, fermenters, clean water |
| **3** | Mechanical fabrication | workshop, power, metalwork, measurement |
| **4** | Controlled chemistry | clean environment, reagents, testing, chemists |
| **5** | Precision synthesis | regulatory approval, sterile plant, labs, scale |

**Rungs cannot be skipped.** Precision synthesis needs the metalwork, power,
water and quality systems of every rung beneath it. This is precisely why
"let us just make our own medicines" fails: it attempts rung 5 from rung 1.

### The candidates, modelled

| Rung | Project | Capex | Net saving/yr | Payback | Viable |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Food staples | $70,000 | +$42,000 | 1.7 yr | yes |
| 1 | Cotton and fibre | $80,000 | +$22,500 | 3.6 yr | yes |
| 1 | Medicinal plants | $25,000 | −$800 | never | **no** |
| 2 | Soap and disinfectant | $20,000 | +$9,750 | 2.0 yr | yes |
| 2 | Compost / biochar fertiliser | $120,000 | +$34,000 | 3.5 yr | yes |
| 2 | Biogas | $90,000 | +$16,750 | 5.4 yr | yes |
| 2 | **Oral rehydration salts** | $18,000 | +$2,300 | 7.8 yr | **yes — lethal** |
| 2 | Bioethanol | $110,000 | +$1,750 | 62.9 yr | marginal |
| 3 | Spare parts fabrication | $90,000 | +$8,750 | 10.3 yr | yes |
| 3 | **Medical oxygen (PSA)** | $105,000 | +$8,000 | 13.1 yr | **yes — lethal** |
| 3 | Solar and batteries | $160,000 | +$8,000 | 20.0 yr | yes |
| 3 | Medical consumables | $70,000 | −$1,500 | never | **no** |
| 4 | IV fluids | $220,000 | −$12,000 | never | **no** |
| 4 | Tablet formulation | $400,000 | −$35,000 | never | **no** |
| 5 | API synthesis | $2,000,000 | −$178,000 | never | **no** |
| 5 | Vaccine manufacture | $5,000,000 | −$400,000 | never | **no** |

**Ten of sixteen are viable. Two of them are lethal-category goods.**

That is the fix: §16 said no lethal import could be localised. Two can.

### The viability test that matters

A project is viable only if **FX saved exceeds the cost of operating the
facility.** This is not pedantry — it is where self-reliance projects
usually die. A plant that saves $40,000 of imports but costs $45,000 a year
to run has made the community **poorer and less resilient**, because now it
needs FX *and* it has a broken factory.

Every unviable row above fails this test, not a test of ambition.

## 17.3 Sovereignty value: why payback is the wrong sole criterion

Ranked purely by payback, medical oxygen sits eleventh and never gets built.
That would be a serious mistake, because financial return does not capture
what oxygen is *for*.

Weighting life-critical goods 5×, the ranking becomes:

| Rank | Project | Sovereignty value | Lethal |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Oral rehydration salts | 1.333 | **yes** |
| 2 | Medical oxygen | 0.810 | **yes** |
| 3 | Food staples | 0.771 | |
| 4 | Soap and disinfectant | 0.638 | |
| 5 | Cotton and fibre | 0.469 | |

### The tradeoff, priced

Two programmes under the same $400,000 budget:

| | Lethal-first | Money-first |
|---|---|---|
| Projects funded | 6 | 6 |
| Net saving | **$101,300/yr** | $127,300/yr |
| Independence gain | 27.6% | **34.4%** |
| **Lethal capabilities** | **2** | **1** |

**Prioritising resilience costs $26,000/year in forgone savings and buys one
additional life-critical capability.**

That is a real price and it should be paid deliberately, not by accident.
The community should be told the number and asked. A programme that quietly
optimises for payback will end up with excellent fertiliser production and
no oxygen — and will discover the problem during a pneumonia outbreak.

## 17.4 Dual-use production: the most valuable category

Four projects produce something ordinary **and** something critical from the
same plant:

| Plant | Ordinary use | Critical use |
|---|---|---|
| Bioethanol still | Cooking and lamp fuel | 70% disinfectant, sterilisation |
| PSA oxygen | Welding, industry | **Medical oxygen** |
| Soap works | Household soap | Infection control, surgical scrub |
| Biogas digester | Cooking, lighting | Clinic power, autoclave heat |

**Dual-use plants are disproportionately valuable, for a reason that is easy
to miss:** the everyday demand keeps the plant running, staffed, maintained
and stocked with spares during normal times. A facility that only operates
in emergencies will be **broken when the emergency arrives** — the operator
has left, the compressor has seized, nobody remembers the procedure.

This is the single most useful design heuristic in the section: **prefer the
plant that earns its keep every day and saves lives occasionally, over the
plant that does nothing until the disaster.**

---

## 17.5 What "indoor farming" can and cannot do

You mentioned petrol, oil, gas and medicine as candidates for indoor
production. Sorting them honestly:

| Claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **Gas** — biogas from waste digestion | **Yes.** Simple, robust, rung 2, uses waste already present |
| **Oil** — plant oils by pressing | **Yes.** Rung 2. Food, soap, lamp fuel, some lubrication |
| **Petrol** — bioethanol as a partial substitute | **Partly.** Runs adapted engines and generators; will not run a diesel truck fleet |
| **Medicine** — see §17.2 | **Split.** Oxygen and ORS yes; APIs and vaccines no |
| **Diesel from algae / synthetic fuels** | **No.** Repeatedly attempted at scale, repeatedly uneconomic |

### The honest caution on fuel

Bioethanol is technically viable and marginal financially — a 62.9-year
payback on FX savings alone. It is worth building anyway, but **for the
dual-use disinfectant value and the fuel-security value, not because it pays
for itself.** Anyone selling a community on bioethanol as a money-maker is
misleading them.

There is also a hard constraint the enthusiasm usually skips: **fuel crops
compete with food crops for land and water.** A district that converts
significant cropland to fuel production has traded a fuel import for a food
import. Cap energy crops at a stated share of arable land — and prefer waste
feedstocks (bagasse, molasses, crop residue) over dedicated planting.

## 17.6 Integration with the currency

Local production and RCU reinforce each other in three specific ways.

**1. New commodity classes appear.** Local oxygen, ethanol, soap, fertiliser
and biogas are all inspectable, storable, weighable goods — exactly the
criteria in §2.1. They can back currency directly. **A community that makes
its own oxygen has added a commodity class that cannot be embargoed.**

**2. Capex can be raised in RCU, not dollars.** A PSA plant costs $105,000 in
hard currency for equipment, but the building, foundations, labour, security
and site works can be paid in commodity notes. **Splitting capex into an FX
component and an RCU component is what makes these projects reachable** —
and it should be an explicit line in every project plan.

**3. Import substitution defends the currency.** Every dollar of imports
displaced is a dollar of export earnings freed for the irreducible basket
(§16.3). The programme above cuts the FX bill 27.6%, which materially
improves the coverage on medicines that genuinely cannot be made locally.

### Revised independence estimate

| | §16 estimate | With production ladder |
|---|---|---|
| Achievable independence | 51.2% | **~60%** |
| Lethal goods locally produced | 0 | **2** |
| Irreducible FX need | $227,000 | ~$180,000 |

Still not independence. But the composition has improved in the way that
matters most: **two things that kill people when they run out are now made
from air, salt and sugar.**

## 17.7 Sequencing

| Phase | Rungs | Projects | Capex |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** (yr 1–3) | 1–2 | Food staples, soap, ORS | ~$110,000 |
| **2** (yr 3–6) | 2–3 | Fertiliser, biogas, **oxygen**, spare parts | ~$405,000 |
| **3** (yr 6–10) | 3 | Solar, textiles, ethanol | ~$350,000 |
| **4** (yr 10+) | 4 | IV fluids — **only** with real regulatory capacity | $220,000+ |
| **Never** | 5 | APIs, vaccines — buy these, forever | — |

**Two rules that should be treated as inviolable:**

1. **Never shut off an import before its local substitute has run reliably
   for twelve months.** This is §16's taper doctrine applied to production.
   A plant that works in commissioning tests is not a supply chain.
2. **Never attempt rung 4 medical production without a qualified pharmacist
   and a real testing lab.** Contaminated IV fluid kills faster than the
   dehydration it treats. This is the one place where enthusiasm is lethal.

---

## 17.8 New risks

| Risk | L | I | Score | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Local plant fails; import already discontinued** | 3 | 5 | **15** | 12-month parallel running before any import is cut |
| **Unsafe local medical production** | 3 | 5 | **15** | Rung 4+ requires pharmacist and lab; ORS and oxygen only below that |
| Plant built, then abandoned for lack of maintenance | 4 | 3 | 12 | Prefer dual-use plants with daily demand; budget maintenance from day one |
| Fuel crops displace food crops | 3 | 4 | 12 | Cap energy crops as share of arable land; prefer waste feedstocks |
| Capex raised, project uneconomic | 3 | 3 | 9 | Viability test: net saving must exceed opex before approval |
| Programme optimises payback, skips lethal goods | 3 | 4 | 12 | Sovereignty-weighted ranking is the default selection rule |

---

## 17.9 Summary

**Your instinct was right and it fixes a real gap.** §16 was too pessimistic
because it treated "medicine" as one thing.

1. **Disaggregate by technical difficulty, not importance.** The production
   ladder has five rungs and they cannot be skipped.
2. **Two lethal-category goods are locally producible today.** Medical oxygen
   from air (~$105k, proven across three East African countries at ~$7.34
   per patient) and oral rehydration salts from sugar and salt (~$18k).
3. **Viability means beating operating cost**, not just displacing imports.
   Six of sixteen candidates fail this and would make the community weaker.
4. **Rank by sovereignty, not payback.** It costs $26,000/year and buys a
   second life-critical capability. Decide it openly.
5. **Prefer dual-use plants.** Everyday demand is what keeps a facility alive
   until the emergency it was built for.
6. **APIs and vaccines stay imported forever.** That limit is real and no
   district-scale effort changes it.

Independence rises from ~51% to ~60%, and — more important than the
percentage — **the things that kill people when they run out are now partly
made from air, salt and sugar.**
