# 19. Military doctrine: the citizen militia model and asymmetric defense

> *"You do not need to destroy the tank. You just need to make it useless.
> A net over a drone and it is finished. Destruction is expensive.
> Neutralization is cheap."*

This document defines the military doctrine for an independent community or
nascent state: a **self-funded, self-equipped, mandatory citizen militia**
engineered for maximum asymmetric resilience.

Where conventional states bankrupt themselves attempting to mirror the
armaments of global superpowers, this doctrine establishes how a distributed,
low-cost defense force renders high-tech military assets irrelevant at cost
ratios exceeding **10,000:1**.

---

## 19.1 Core design principles

Every modern military relies on concentrated capital, industrial supply chains,
and hierarchical logistics tails. This model rejects all three in favor of
distributed population resilience:

| Principle | What it means | Strategic rationale |
|---|---|---|
| **Self-funded** | Every fighter purchases and maintains their own kit, weapons, rations, and gear | Eliminates military budget corruption; zero defense contractor lobbying; zero foreign arms dependency |
| **Mandatory registration** | Every citizen (ages 18–50) is enrolled on a community register | Prevents free-riding; provides complete situational accounting of personnel, skills, and assets |
| **Self-funded transport** | Fighters arrange their own movement (horses, trucks, bicycles, foot) | Eliminates vulnerable fuel convoys and logistics tails; no central transport fleet to bomb |
| **Self-preparation** | Fighters store and maintain readiness gear at home | Distributes defense across thousands of locations; eliminates centralized bases that can be destroyed |
| **Centralized training** | Standardized doctrine and tactical training provided by community authority | Ensures common tactical language, safety, and small-unit cohesion despite varied personal gear |
| **No mirror-copying** | If the enemy builds tanks, do not build tanks; if they fly jets, do not buy jets | Mirroring guarantees defeat against wealthier adversaries; asymmetry is the only path to survival |
| **Medieval & low-tech weapons** | Swords, bows, spears, crossbows, horses, caltrops, traps are fully integrated | Decouples defense from ammunition factories and foreign supply chains; a blade never runs out of bullets |

## 19.2 Historical precedent: proof of the asymmetric model

This model is not theoretical. It has repeatedly defeated the largest empires
and professional standing armies in human history:

### 1. The Minutemen (American Revolutionary War, 1775)
- **Self-funded & equipped:** Farmers brought their own hunting rifles, powder horns, horses, and rations.
- **Mandatory enrollment:** Colonial statute required every able-bodied adult male to own a firearm and drill.
- **Tactics:** Refused open-field European linear formations; engaged British columns from behind trees, stone walls, and hedgerows with high-precision marksmanship.
- **Outcome:** Expelled and defeated the premier global military power of the 18th century.

### 2. The Boer Commandos (South Africa, 1899–1902)
- **Self-funded & mounted:** Every burgher supplied his own Mauser rifle, ammunition bandoliers, horse, and biltong.
- **Universal service:** Mandatory mobilization of men aged 16 to 60 organized into local commandos under elected field cornets.
- **Asymmetric mobility:** 40,000 mounted farmers outmaneuvered, ambushed, and tied down 500,000 British Empire troops with heavy artillery and armored trains for three years.
- **Outcome:** Inflicted devastating losses; forced the British into costly scorched-earth tactics and concentration camps that wrecked their international standing.

### 3. The Finnish Ski Troops (Winter War, 1939–1940)
- **Self-equipped for climate:** Finnish soldiers used their own cross-country skis, white camouflage smocks, and hunting rifles.
- **Tactics (*Motti*):** Skied silently through subarctic forests where 6,000 Soviet tanks could not navigate. Encirled and chopped Soviet mechanized columns into isolated pockets.
- **Outcome:** Inflicted over 300,000 Soviet casualties while suffering ~25,000 losses, preserving Finnish sovereignty against an invasion force of 1 million men.

### 4. The Afghan Mujahideen (1979–1989 and 2001–2021)
- **Decentralized logistics:** Fighters operated from mountain villages, relying on personal rifles, pack mules, donkeys, and local food stores.
- **Terrain exploitation:** Used complex cave systems and mountain passes where armored divisions and attack helicopters could not establish control.
- **Outcome:** Defeated both the Soviet Union (10-year war leading to imperial collapse) and the United States ($2+ trillion spent over 20 years resulting in strategic withdrawal).

### 5. The Zulu Impis (Southern Africa, 1816–1879)
- **Self-supplied & organized:** Warriors provided their own cowhide shields (*isihlangu*), stabbing spears (*iklwa*), and cattle rations from their homesteads.
- **Mobility & doctrine:** Regiments marched up to 80 km per day on foot; executed the "horns of the buffalo" (*izimpondo zankomo*) double envelopment.
- **Outcome:** At the Battle of Isandlwana (1879), annihilated a modern British column of 1,800 professional soldiers armed with Martini-Henry breechloading rifles and artillery using spears and tactical discipline.

### A. The Register: Mandatory Enrollment

1. **Eligibility:** All citizens, male and female, aged 18–50.
2. **Recording mechanism:** Dual recordkeeping — encrypted offline mobile ledger synchronized during periodic community council meetings, backed by an indelible physical paper ledger stored in a fireproof archive.
3. **Recorded data:** Identity, age, residential coordinates, physical condition, specialized capabilities (medical, mechanical, blacksmithing, marksmanship, communication, cavalry), verified weapons owned, transport available, and mobilization station.
4. **Enforcement & non-registration penalty:** Loss of access to community cooperative resources (RCU currency issuance, warehouse grain reserves, free medical clinic access, apprenticeship programmes). Free-riding on collective defense is met with economic exclusion from collective production.
5. **Exemptions:** Severe medical disability (certified by the community medical council), advanced pregnancy, nursing/childcare for children under 2, and extreme infirmity. Exemptions are reviewed annually.

#### What Training Explicitly Excludes:
- Marching in parade formation, ceremonial saluting, polishing brass and boots.
- Heavy tank driving, fixed-wing flight training, naval maneuvers.
- Frontal assaults, human-wave charges, static trench holding under artillery barrages.

#### What Training Emphasizes:
1. **Precision marksmanship:** Every fighter must reliably strike a man-sized target at 200 meters with iron sights.
2. **Field craft & camouflage:** Silent movement, utilizing negative dead space, building concealed spider holes, visual and thermal signature reduction.
3. **7-day survival:** Independent water sourcing, fire concealment (Dakota fire pits), navigation by stars and terrain contour.
4. **Point-of-injury medicine:** Tourniquet application in under 30 seconds, chest seals, wound packing, managing hemorrhagic shock.
5. **Small-unit tactics (3–5 person teams):** Bounding overwatch, fire-and-movement, hasty ambushes, coordinated break-contact drills.
6. **Asymmetric warfare:** IED fabrication, roadway sabotages, decoy construction, urban sniping, blending with non-combatant populations.
7. **Mounted operations:** Riding, mounted scouting, packing pack saddles for rapid offloading.
8. **Low-tech & medieval weapons:** Formations with spears, rapid-fire recurve archery, crossbow reloading under pressure, caltrop scattering.

## 19.4 Operational doctrine: how the force fights

The citizen militia never engages in conventional force-on-force battles. It
operates through five interrelated strategic concepts:

### 1. The Swarm
- Hundreds of independent 3–5 fighter cells converge on an enemy element simultaneously from 360 degrees, attack for 90–180 seconds, and disperse before artillery or air support can be coordinated.
- The adversary cannot concentrate heavy fires because there is no single front line or headquarters to target.

### 2. The Bleed
- Every culvert contains an IED; every rooftop holds a sniper; every fuel convoy faces roadblocks and ambushes.
- Victory does not require destroying the enemy army in a day; it requires making every single 24-hour period of occupation cost the enemy more blood, treasure, and morale than they can sustain.

### 3. The Ghost
- Fighters wear standard civilian work clothes. By day they farm, mill flour, and repair engines; by night they deploy weapons and execute ambushes.
- The occupying army cannot target the militia without targeting the entire civilian population, triggering domestic and international political collapse.

### 4. The Fortress
- All critical command nodes, RCU commodity vaults (§3), medical triage clinics (§15), and ammunition caches are constructed underground, in caves, or inside reinforced tunnels.
- Heavy aerial bombardment strikes empty topsoil while the community's assets remain completely intact.

### 5. The Porcupine
- The community postures itself so that conquering it offers minimal economic reward while exacting unacceptable casualties and costs.
- The adversary calculates that swallowing the porcupine is fatal, and chooses not to attack.

## 19.6 Neutralizing superior technology

> **Core Insight:** *A $10 million tank or a $150 million stealth fighter is
> an intricate, fragile system. You do not need to blow up 60 tons of armor
> plating to defeat a tank; you only need to blind its $5,000 camera or starve
> its engine of air.*

### Part I: The Main Battle Tank Problem

#### What a Tank Actually Is:
- A 60-ton sealed steel box with blind periscopes and external optical glass.
- Deafened by its own 1,500 horsepower turbine/diesel engine (120+ dB).
- Immobile if a single track pin breaks or drive sprocket jams.
- Consumes 3–5 liters of fuel per kilometer; disabled when fuel runs dry.
- Manned by 3–4 vulnerable humans who cannot sleep, eat, or see inside a hot metal box under siege.

#### Low-Cost Tank Neutralization Methods:

| Method | Est. Cost | Tactical Mechanism | Operational Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sensor blinding** | $2–10 | Weighted nets, mud buckets, or paint cans hurled at periscopes and thermal optics | **Tank blinded;** cannot acquire targets; crew must halt or open hatch |
| **Track jamming** | $0–20 | Heavy steel rebar, logging chains, or hardwood logs wedged into drive sprocket | **Tank immobilized;** thrown track; becomes stationary target |
| **Anti-belly ditch** | $0 (labor) | 2m wide by 3m deep concealed trench across approach corridor | **Tank high-centered;** tracks spin in air; impossible to self-recover |
| **Fuel starvation** | $50 | Ambushes and IEDs targeted exclusively against soft-skinned fuel tankers | **Tank starved;** 60-ton metal box abandoned by crew within 48 hours |
| **Air intake choking** | $2–5 | Wet foam, thick tarps, or sand thrown over rear engine air intake grilles | **Engine suffocates & overheats;** turbine stalls; vehicle shuts down |
| **Engine deck Molotovs** | $2–5 | Glass bottles of gasoline/oil thrown onto rear engine louvers | **Engine fire;** melts hydraulic lines; triggers automatic fire systems |
| **Smoke & dust screens** | $5–20 | Burning tires and damp vegetation creating dense particulate clouds | **Optics degraded;** thermal and laser rangefinders blinded |
| **Acoustic disorientation**| $0 | Sirens, firecrackers, and hull hammering at night | **Crew sleep deprivation;** situational panic and cognitive failure |
| **Magnetic hull charges** | $10–50 | Shaped charges attached to tracks or hull bottom during night crawls | **Track link severed;** road wheels shattered |
| **Waterway flooding** | $0 (labor) | Breaching irrigation dykes or canal gates across approach routes | **Tank bogged;** sinks into soft silt beyond recovery |

#### The Open-Field Tank Engagement in 5 Phases:
1. **Preparation:** Network of camouflaged spider holes and pre-positioned wire/net caches dug into the field.
2. **Approach:** Armored column enters the open field; defenders hold fire and let lead vehicles enter the kill zone.
3. **Simultaneous Blinding:** On whistle command, defenders rise from spider holes; weighted nets and paint cover all optical blocks; smoke generators ignited.
4. **Immobilization:** Rebar and heavy chains thrown into tracks of trailing tanks; Molotovs strike lead engine decks.
5. **Surrender / Capture:** Trapped, blind, immobile crews open hatches and surrender intact vehicles without a single anti-tank missile fired.

### Part II: The Next-Gen Fighter / Stealth Jet Problem (F-22 / F-35)

#### What a Stealth Fighter Actually Is:
- A $150 million machine flying at 1,000+ km/h that cannot hover or loiter.
- Cannot see an individual human hiding beneath dense tree canopy or underground.
- Requires a 2,500m pristine paved runway, complex ground crews, and thousands of liters of JP-8 fuel per sortie.
- Carries only 4–8 internal missiles per flight before being forced to return to base.

#### Low-Cost Fighter Neutralization Methods:

| Method | Est. Cost | Mechanism | Operational Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Runway cratering** | $50–200 | Sabotage teams plant IEDs or plow trenches across regional airbase runways | **Fighter grounded;** high-performance jets cannot take off or land |
| **Fuel depot sabotage** | $20–100 | Incendiary strikes against bulk JP-8 fuel storage tanks and pumping stations | **Fighter stranded;** aircraft cannot fly without specialized fuel |
| **Dispersal & camouflage** | $0 | Dispersing fighters into caves, tree cover, and decentralized bunkers | **Fighter irrelevant;** radar finds no massed targets to strike |
| **Thermal decoys** | $5–50 | Steel drums burning waste wood to create false heat signatures | **Missiles wasted;** enemy expends $1.5M precision bombs on $10 decoys |
| **Weather exploitation** | $0 | Launching operations during heavy rain, fog, dust storms, or thick cloud cover | **Sensors degraded;** precision laser/optical targeting rendered ineffective |
| **Night operations** | $0 | Tactical movements conducted exclusively during peak darkness | **Visual acquisition denied;** air support effectiveness drops drastically |
| **Underground hardening** | $0 (labor) | Constructing command posts, triage centers, and armories beneath 3m of earth | **Bombs ineffective;** surface strikes hit empty dirt |
| **Ground crew interdiction**| $0 | Harassing sniper fires and mortar ambushes against airbase maintenance personnel | **Fighter grounded;** aircraft cannot launch without 20–30 technicians |

> **The Historical Lesson of Air Power:** *Air power has never defeated a
> dispersed, hidden, determined ground force. The US dropped 7.5 million tons
> of bombs on Vietnam and 80,000+ tons on Afghanistan over 20 years. In both
> cases, the dispersed ground force won.*

## 19.8 Honest caveats and limitations

A doctrine that ignores its own vulnerabilities is dangerous fantasy. The
honest limitations of this model must be recognized and planned for:

1. **Strictly defensive:** This force cannot invade another country, capture
   distant territory, or project power abroad. It exists solely to make its
   own home district unconquerable.
2. **Adversary adaptation:** Once the adversary realizes tanks are being blinded
   by nets, they will weld mesh screens over periscopes and install perimeter
   thermal sirens. The militia must continually innovate its low-tech tactics.
3. **Severe casualties:** Asymmetric war against an industrialized adversary is
   brutal. Even degraded precision weapons cause real bloodshed. People will
   die, and communities must possess deep civic resolve.
4. **Open-field vulnerability:** Open terrain heavily favors high-tech sensors,
   radar, and aerial attack. Open-field engagements should be avoided whenever
   possible. Defense must exploit forests, mountains, broken gullies, bad weather,
   and night cover.
5. **Political victory is the ultimate objective:** The citizen militia never
   expects to destroy 100% of the invading army. The objective is to prolong
   the campaign, raise the cost-per-day beyond acceptable thresholds, and force
   the adversary's domestic political leadership to abandon the war.

## 19.9 Summary in one paragraph

The **Citizen Militia Model** is a mandatory, self-funded, and self-equipped
defense system drawn from the entire adult population (ages 18–50). Every
